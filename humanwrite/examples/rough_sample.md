Should We Let Kids Fail?

I think we teach kids so hard to not fail that we stop them from learning what failure is for. My little brother dropped out of his soccer team last year and I thought that was the worst thing that could happen to him, but he came back in the spring and he was way better than before. He told me that the coaches made him practice alone for two months and that was the best thing they could have done for him. He said he hated every minute of it, and then he said he was glad they did it. I did not believe him at the time but now I kind of do. I think that is how you actually get better, by doing the thing you hate on your own until it stops being scary.

When a kid does bad on a test, most of the time the first thing that happens is someone fixes it for them. My mom used to call my teachers when I got a C and she would be nice about it, but the teachers could tell she was mad. I got better grades that year but I dont think I learned anything from it. The C would have taught me more than any of the A's I got after that, probably. I remember sitting in the car and my mom saying "you can do better than that" and I was nodding and I was not even listening. That was the moment I stopped trying to earn the C and started trying to earn her call. I think that is backwards.

In the book "The Glass Castle," Jeannette's dad never protected her from failing. She went hungry, she froze in cold, and her brother used to leave her alone in a window at night. That was rough but it made her tough. She says on page 96 that her father "was a man with a plan" and I think that plan included letting them hit the ground hard so they would know how to get back up. On page 121 she writes about how her family could not afford heat one winter and they just got used to it, and I think getting used to cold is a kind of failing that makes you strong. The kids in that house never got a redo.

Some people will say that is too harsh and I get that. Not every kid has the same home as she did. But I think there is a middle ground where we let kids struggle a little more. We could stop doing so much of the homework for them, we could let the team sit them out for a week, and we could let them fail a project without a redo. The redo is the part that makes them weak, not the failing. My cousin got a redo on her science fair project after it lost and she got second place, and I think second place after a redo is worse than last place the first time because you never knew if you could come back.

There is another part of this that people miss which is that failing in front of people is harder than failing alone. When I bombed the spelling bee in fifth grade everyone in the cafeteria knew about it by lunch. That was the worst week of my life and also, honestly, the week I decided I would not be that kid again. I think schools are afraid of that week for their students. They would rather have nobody stand out at all than have one kid get laughed at. I dont know if that is fair to the kids who actually want to stand out.

The thing about rescuing kids all the time is they stop knowing what they can handle. I saw this with my brother because he was the only one on the team who got sent to practice alone, and he came back and he did not care what anyone thought. The other kids still got mad when they lost a game and made faces at the refs, but he just shook hands. I think he had already lost so many times by himself that losing in public stopped being a big deal. That is a skill and I dont know how to teach it any other way.

My mom would not agree with me on this. She thinks I am talking about something that did not happen to her and I am right about that. She had a teacher who failed her on purpose to make her work harder and she talks about it like it was a bad thing. Maybe it was. But I think the lesson stuck with her for a long time and that is what I want. I want the lesson to stick, even if it hurts a little when it lands. I dont want my kids to have a perfect record and no memory of what it felt like to miss.

So I think we should let kids fail more. They are not going to die from it and some of them are actually going to get better because of it. I dont think we need a perfect system, I just think we need to stop being so scared of the C. The C is where the learning is. Everything else is just the part where they show up and smile for the picture.
